

Mosquitoes have shaped societies worldwide by transmitting deadly diseases such as malaria and yellow fever, with some estimates suggesting that they have killed nearly half of all people who have ever lived. In response, humanity has fought back over the past century through innovations in mosquito control, beginning with scientifically targeted habitat control and expanding to large-scale chemical interventions.

The history of mosquito control reveals a recurring cycle of innovation, success, and setbacks. Despite their major impact on public health, successful interventions encountered biological, ecological, and societal challenges that influenced subsequent innovations and prompted changes in control strategies.

This historical cycle has scientific, ecological, and ethical implications for future innovations, including emerging genetic appraoches that aim to suppress mosquito populations or reduce their ability to transmit disease.
